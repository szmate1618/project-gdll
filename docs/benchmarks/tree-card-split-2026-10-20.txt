Geometry and preparation
- Healthy four-card broadleaf and pine assets only. Shrubs, dry trees and top
  cards retain authored geometry. Classification happens once at GPU upload.
- Source foliage green and row width estimate the trunk/canopy boundary.
  Alpha below 16/255 is ignored, reduced if needed for a lower material cutoff.
  Four texels of padding surround the trunk crop; roots remain included.
- Unsupported geometry/UV/attributes and negligible savings keep the original.
- Original indices are retained for --no-tree-card-splitting; both replacement
  quads share one instanced draw. No per-frame scan or extra draw calls.
- Each changed upright card goes from 2 to 4 triangles. Full healthy assets go
  from 8 to 12 triangles; distant side billboards go from 2 to 4. Top LoD stays 2.
- Camera and shadow tree draws use the same toggle. Bounds, placement matrices,
  collision capsules, textures and existing culling/batching are unchanged.

Image checks
- Synthetic nearest-filtered GPU captures are pixel-identical before/after,
  including reflected asset nodes, near four-card and distant side/top LoDs.
- Actual map captures were compared and visually inspected with a 32x diff.
  Mean absolute channel differences range from 0.000030 to 0.005485 on 0..255.
  At most nine pixels per view change by more than 16 in any channel.
- No visible trunk clipping or split seam in the inspected views. Captures are
  not pixel-identical: retriangulation/filtering and depth rounding can differ.
  Four texels cannot conservatively cover all coarse mip footprints.

Assessment
The clearest win is the 100 m forest view: GPU 9.79 -> 9.02 ms (~7.8%), with
all six paired round averages faster. Forest A and B improve about 2.2% and
3.0%; other views are nearly flat or slightly slower. This is a small, local
fill-rate optimization rather than a general renderer speedup. Extra triangles
can offset the reduced transparent area. Enabled for trying in the built game;
use --no-tree-card-splitting for the original geometry.
