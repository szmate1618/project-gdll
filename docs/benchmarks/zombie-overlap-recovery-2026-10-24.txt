Low-rate traveler overlap recovery and route progress — 2026-10-24
=================================================================

Decision and user criterion
---------------------------
Road overlap alone is not a rejection criterion. The user accepts imperfect road
geometry while that geometry awaits cleanup, provided zombies keep making
progress. Evaluate sustained stalls separately from overlap counts.

Keep the game's retained swept movement unchanged. The original low-rate variant
accumulates many additional stalled routes and loses its short-run performance
advantage. An explicit repair-and-restore prototype recovers overlaps, but adds
position jumps and does not eliminate navigation stalls. A cheap height-envelope
guard is a promising faster candidate, but also stalls some actors which the
retained version moves successfully. None of these measurements justify claiming
that an automatic physics solver will eventually fix walking zombies.

All prototypes, harness sources and binaries are isolated under ignored build/
artifacts; no production source/header was changed or restored. Base: 12cc001,
experiment/zombie-distant-travel-20261024. This follow-up measures alternatives;
it does not introduce new gameplay flags or change the default movement policy.

Protocol
--------
Same real map, navmesh, assets and seed-42 scenario as the earlier report:
1,600 zombies, stationary player X=-526.465, Z=296.095. All simulation updates
advance a fixed 1/60 s, independent of real elapsed time. No rendering, audio,
networking or dynamic-physics advancement in these headless cases. Door and prop
queries see fixed live poses. Each quality run follows 60 simulated seconds:
3,600 frames /5,760,000 actor-step samples. Thirteen serial quality invocations
include repeated variants for progress tracking. Tests cover this fixture, not
all maps, moving interactions or arbitrarily long travel.

Timed runs are separate from quality diagnostics. CPU 0, release compiler flags,
no fine-query timers. Ten serial invocations, in F/S/R/E/W/W/E/R/S/F order.
Each invocation resets the population three times, warms 60 updates and records
3,600 updates per reset. Each variant has 21,600 timed updates covering repeated
61-second trajectories with the first second excluded from timings. Loading and
route planning are excluded. No builds, other experiments or heavy analysis run
alongside timed cases. No power/CPU/GPU settings were changed.

All 23 monitored invocations (quality + timing) began/ended on balanced power;
all 421 two-second power samples reported AC online. This does not guarantee
fixed hardware clocks. All invocations exited successfully. The isolated
original and retained implementations reproduced their earlier six-second final
poses byte for byte before these longer measurements. Current headers and
compiled library layouts were used throughout, without timestamp restoration.

The diagnostic timing.csv files also contain standalone key=value counter
records emitted outside the timed scope. Analysis filters those records and
requires exactly 10,800 timed rows per invocation. Do not treat those counter
records as extra frames. Per-run results and the filtering script are preserved.

Variants
--------
F /full: current exact movement model at every frame, with the retained exact
navigation/contact-region optimizations; distant approximation disabled.
S /swept: current shipped experimental default, low-rate distant segment refresh
with the conservative swept guard and current ground/crowd/navmesh validation.
R /reduced: reconstructed original rejected prototype: 10 Hz steering/endpoint
capsule validation, with per-frame feet/ground/crowd/navmesh updates.
E /envelope: R plus the same current-ground endpoint-height envelope guard used
by S; unexpected height excursions trigger full movement. No simplified swept
wall/trunk test and no periodic overlap-repair query.
W /rewind10: R plus staggered 10 Hz current-body overlap checks. On overlap, try
up to four horizontal penetration corrections with ground resampling, at most
30 cm total, and validate coverage/crowd. If push-out cannot produce clear feet,
revalidate and restore a recently checked clear position within 30 cm. Then use
full movement for 250 ms. Recovery can visibly jump and move backward.

Additional quality-only variants detect overlap at 10/5 Hz and switch to full
movement, or add bounded push-out at 10/5 Hz without the last-clear restore.
They are not exposed through the game's APIs or CLI.

What counts as penetration and a stall
--------------------------------------
Physical body contacts use the original capsule (radius .3 m, height 1.8 m),
zero skin AND zero extra wall padding, with the existing walking support
exemptions and .1 mm depth tolerance. Separate diagnostics record zero-skin
contacts with the 5 cm wall margin and padded .002 m contacts. The previous
zero-skin validation retained the wall margin; separating it does not change
the 12-overlapping-actor result at six seconds in this fixture.

Contact.terrain groups terrain and roads. Other contacts are reported separately.
Source-mesh inspection identified both non-terrain/road witnesses in R as
railways_ballast triangles at two different locations. They are ground
infrastructure too; do not describe them as demonstrated wall/tree penetrations.
Inspection used each actor's last recorded non-terrain/road contact pose; this is
not a per-contact source annotation for every earlier frame.
Actor 727 has a long ballast overlap and little final progress even in the full
and retained controls; actor 1101 continues moving through a ballast overlap.
Overlap alone therefore cannot diagnose a new navigation stall.

Consecutive overlapping actor frames form episodes. Durations are observed frame
counts /60, with one-frame temporal resolution. Episodes still open at the final
frame are right-censored; their observed duration is a lower bound on eventual
duration. Four W episodes remain open at the cutoff, each observed <=100 ms.
Do not claim those four were measured to recover after the cutoff.

Primary progress metric: unfinished scenario route, goal farther than 1 m,
less than 5 cm net horizontal movement over the final five simulated seconds.
This detects stationary actors and near-stationary jitter, but remains a stall
proxy; it does not prove every obstacle wait is a bug. Compare actor IDs too:
equal totals can conceal different stalled routes. A secondary metric counts
actors continuously moving less than 1 mm/frame for the final two seconds.
Original behavior/finished routes are excluded from these progress metrics.

Merely detecting an overlap and switching to the normal movement path does not
ensure recovery; it can interrupt a coarse segment that would have escaped.
The normal solver limits correction by requested movement, and zero requested
movement returns without depenetration. Explicit push-out can fail on support
geometry. Restoring validated clear feet handles the failed-push cases at the
cost of backward motion and occasional larger displacement.

Crossings and remaining limits
-------------------------------
An independent diagnostic tests body-axis motion at nine heights through nearby
steep static triangles, and crossings of actual trunk cylindrical cores. R has
three static crossing actor-frames, all source-identified road meshes:
  frame 85, actor 463: roads_dirt
  frame 1209, actor 1: roads_dirt
  frame 3262, actor 1409: roads_asphalt
No other variant has a detected static-axis crossing; none has a trunk-core
crossing. These are sampled body-axis/core witnesses, not a complete continuous
capsule proof for every barrier/rounded trunk cap. All quality cases stay in the
navmesh; only explicit push/restore variants exceed normal motion speed bounds.
Every population makes exactly 1,600 startup path requests and no runtime path
searches. No per-frame ground/crowd checks were removed from the prototypes.

The F/S controls already have low-progress routes; their causes need separate
investigation. Periodic collision repair does not guarantee route completion.
Accepting overlap on roads or railway ballast is reasonable as a visual tradeoff,
but it does not by itself remove the observed stalls. Under the user's progress
criterion, prioritize diagnosing those stalled actors or a targeted recovery
policy rather than rejecting a variant solely for shallow road overlap.
The cheaper E candidate merits follow-up only with stalled-route recovery or
additional evidence; W's modest speed gain must justify its jumps and complexity.
No rendering/visible-motion improvement or behavior on moving props is claimed.
