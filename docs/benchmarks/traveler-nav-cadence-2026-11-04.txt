Distant fallback navigation cadence, 2026-11-04
===============================================

Implementation
--------------
Distant travelers retain a navigation velocity and bounded endpoint for up to
100 ms during physical fallback. The full scene/crowd movement solver, exact
ground support and actual accepted-step navmesh guard still run every step.
The ordinary nearby-player and --full-scenario-movement paths remain full rate.
The 200/160 m multiplayer-aware distance hysteresis is unchanged.

Fallback reuses an already sampled coarse command where valid. Commands expire
by time, not by physical progress: blocked distance is never caught up later.
Movement is bounded by 1.2 m/s and the sampled endpoint plane. A close float
corner/goal can finish only within the current frame's speed budget. Multi-part
frames retain the complete start-to-final chord guard.

World revisions, external feet changes, route generations, looking, mode changes
and ordinary-behavior takeover invalidate cached intent. A rejected actual move
retries a frame-sized navigation sample immediately. Numerical recovery can
retain frame-sized sampling through the short fallback cooldown; it retries the
coarse cadence when the normal segment planner runs again. This avoids repeatedly
trying the same failing long sample every frame. No runtime path searches or
new behavior states are introduced. Dogs use their existing independent system.

TravelerPlan grows from 56 to 88 bytes on this build: approximately 32 KiB for
1,000 routes. No cache is allocated for the other dormant population slots.

Benchmark setup
---------------
AMD Ryzen 5 7535HS, Linux, Release C++17 (-O3 -DNDEBUG), pinned to CPU 0.
Scenario: scenarios/gray-scott-corridors/scenario.toml, seed 42.
Observer X/Z (-700,300), eye Y 6.99757 m. 53,000 authored zombies, including
1,000 travelers, plus 10,000 dormant interior slots; zero dogs. 155,825 trunks
and 1,157,182 collision triangles. Static doors/prop poses, no physics advance,
rendering, audio or networking.
Map SHA256: 97c1af98601cc01a0351e0ba1eb128330e026f3823481a1ae3ab24c293ea1030

Each aged run advances 68,460 unmeasured steps followed by 3,600 measured steps,
all exactly 1/60 s: the measured window is approximately minute 19-20 of the
same seeded scenario. One round alternates 61-step unprofiled/profiled blocks,
giving 1,830 control and 1,770 detailed samples. The block length rotates relative
to the 6-step navigation and 16-step fallback cycles. Compilation/tests and other
benchmarks do not run during timing. Original routes match byte for byte.

The saved control executable uses legacy per-step fallback navigation, with
internally matching headers/libraries. Its source override is in a flattened
scratch directory without historical headers. Both its short and entire aged
final trajectories match the original implementation byte for byte, including
the earlier twenty-minute investigation. The accepted variant uses current
production code. This is an approximate distant movement policy; its trajectory
is not required to equal the control's. Short control/detailed runs within the
accepted variant remain identical.

Physical work is not skipped. Detailed body-movement calls increase from 814.74
to 876.54 per step, crowd sweeps from 877.53 to 945.33, and contact queries from
1,747.82 to 1,981.11, including sample-boundary slices and guarded retries.
Numerical recovery remains full rate where necessary, so aggregate navigation
calls do not fall to precisely one sixth of the baseline.

The short startup window is essentially unchanged: unprofiled means are 1.0058
and .9907 ms/step. Fine profiling has substantial overhead; use unprofiled totals
for overall CPU performance. These headless results do not predict a whole-viewer
FPS improvement or replay the user's moving observer, ragdolls and GPU load.

Progress and safety
-------------------
Among the same 1,000 original travelers, both aged variants complete 66 routes.
Mean straight-line distance remaining to the original goal decreases from
2,514.67 to 2,486.86 m. Mean locomotion clock increases from 552.08 to 577.99 s;
this clock is a progress indicator, not a precise distance when behavior changes.
Normal perception takes over seven routes in the control and eight in the
accepted variant, leaving 927 and 926 active routes respectively.

Both have zero active travelers outside the navmesh, no negative ground-support
errors, and three final world-contact flags (different actors). Maximum positive
support offsets are .272047 and .271979 m; rounded-foot ledge support can retain
feet above the surface beneath the center, so this is not unsupported descent.

Crowd quality is approximate: the minimum final traveler/neighbor horizontal
gap is .599889 m in the control and .597665 m in the accepted variant, for a
nominal .600 m diameter. One accepted-variant pair overlaps by about 2.3 mm
(IDs 52521/30086); neither variant has an overlap exceeding 1 cm. This difference
is recorded rather than claiming collision-identical trajectories. Measurements
use full-precision binary roots, not the rounded quality CSV coordinates.

Full project build succeeds. Thirteen focused/integration CTest suites pass.
New regressions cover moving crowd members and boxes, closing/reopening doors,
route reset/replacement, world replacement, no catch-up speed, and forced
fallback through float portal corners. Existing nearby-player and comparison-mode
checks continue to require full-rate navigation.

Rejected candidate
------------------
The first candidate also checked the proposed step before physical resolution.
It reduced steering calls but increased native total time to 4.7993 ms/step and
coverage to 2.4695 ms/step. That redundant proposal check and repeated long-sample
retries were rejected. The accepted implementation validates the actual solved
movement and retains frame-sized sampling during numerical recovery. First-pass
data remain preserved under cached-short/ and cached-aged/ in the artifact root.
