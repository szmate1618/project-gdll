Isolated rendering optimizations, 2026-10-26
Baseline: master 3e02ce9. Candidates change one responsibility each.
Hardware: AMD Radeon 660M, radeonsi/rembrandt, Mesa 24.2.8.
Release build; OpenGL 3.3 requested, hardware context reports 4.6; 1280x720.

Method
All executables were rebuilt from matching source/header layouts and retained
with their own shader directories. Restored C++ files received fresh mtimes.
No builds or other tests ran concurrently with the final timing comparisons.
Map cases alternate baseline/candidate process order over six pairs, with
48 completed warmup frames and 128 measured frames per view per process.
GPU timestamps are asynchronous with at most four frames in flight. Warmup
completion, query draining, captures and file output are outside CPU timers.
Maps/cameras are static; no AI, audio, player updates or dynamic physics runs.
Vegetation nearest filtering, card splitting and the 1024 sunlight map remain
enabled. Both variants use the same settings, assets and 60 Hz animation input.

Numbers are milliseconds. Map absolute values are medians of process-round
means. Savings are medians of paired percentage changes, so percentages need
not equal the ratio of the two displayed medians. Positive savings mean faster.
CPU render includes driver stalls. CPU and GPU overlap: never add them.
GPU clocks, temperature and background activity are uncontrolled. These are
workload-specific stage measurements, not end-to-end gameplay FPS.

1. Disabled flashlight calculations and unused driving shadow pass
The old and new code both have flashlight shadows enabled in driving cases.
Driving already suppresses the handheld flashlight color; the candidate skips
its unused shadow map and shader calculations. Headlights retain their light.
This is an old-code/new-code comparison, not shadows enabled versus disabled.
Flashlight shadows remain optional and default off.

The driving cases improve in all six pairs. The zero-color shader check alone
has only a small, inconsistent effect in the off controls; no standalone gain
is established for that guard. Flashlight-on controls are approximately flat.

2. Deferred zombie animation frame calculation
The fixture updates 100,000 synthetic walk/run actors on a fixed 1/60 s timeline.
Each of three external process pairs contains eight alternating visible/hidden
rounds, with 16 warmup and 128 measured updates. Only Renderer::updateZombies()
is timed. Caller updates, bounds changes, rendering and output are excluded.
The synthetic marker keeps the real update/preparation APIs without asset load
or GPU draw cost. This is not a 100,000-active-AI or real-character FPS claim.
External process runs are independent samples; inner rounds are descriptive.

case | CPU preparation baseline -> candidate | paired saving | faster pairs
hidden | 6.108 -> 3.821 | +37.6% | 3/3
visible | 6.374 -> 3.857 | +38.2% | 3/3

Final published walk/run poses and visible captures match exactly. Hidden
actors do not publish a camera pose. Animation timing is now evaluated and
validated when a pose is needed for rendering, including shadow-only actors.
A separate all-map control pair has identical captures and geometry counts.

Six uniforms and the resolved unit-0 texture binding are cached. Culling and
winding remain explicit because individual layers override them. Source draw
order is unchanged. Uploads, frame/pass boundaries and external opaque draws
invalidate the relevant state.

Standard zombies-open-100k-v1 controls
Every measured frame submits all 100,000 actors, ten actual character variants,
normal LOD3, ten draws and 12,580,000 camera triangles. No sun-shadow casters
are in range. All captures match; all runs have 384/384 finite GPU samples.
Values below are median round means, not paired speedup claims.

run | GPU ms | CPU render ms
baseline-before | 18.799 | 22.357
flashlight | 22.926 | 27.755
animation | 21.623 | 26.182
material | 21.626 | 25.600
combined | 21.937 | 25.589
baseline-after | 21.973 | 26.034

The early/late baseline GPU difference is about 17%; it exceeds most variant
differences. Combined GPU time matches the late baseline. These idle-crowd
controls establish matching output/population, not a crowd rendering speedup.
The animation change targets changed walking actors and does not accelerate
this idle control. Two extra alternating baseline/flashlight crowd pairs retain identical
captures and populations. Results (median of process-round means):
gpu_ms: 21.283 -> 21.063 ms; paired saving +1.7%; 2/2 pairs faster.
cpu_render_ms: 25.067 -> 25.152 ms; paired saving +0.3%; 1/2 pairs faster.
The follow-up does not reproduce a crowd GPU slowdown. Two pairs do not
establish a general crowd speedup.
