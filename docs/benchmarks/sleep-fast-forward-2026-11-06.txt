Sleep fast-forward and approximate travellers, 2026-11-06
======================================================

Behaviour
---------
Accepted players sleep after group agreement. Guards retain ordinary play at
1x and can join sleep later. All sleeping permits a target of 30x. Physics keeps
its 1/60 s ticks, inputs are acknowledged without applying sleeping players'
gameplay actions, and snapshots retain 20 Hz of wall time. Sleepers skip world
rendering. The standalone viewer repeats its fixed-step callbacks too.

Accelerated travellers advance along individual stored waypoint paths every
.5 simulation seconds at 1.2 m/s. They skip steering, physical/crowd collision
and ground sampling. Every crossed leg checks the 60 m sleeper boundary,
including intermediate corners. A threatening slice is withheld and wakes the
group, potentially up to .6 m early. Waking restores terrain height before
publishing awake state; there are no runtime path searches. This intentionally
approximates offscreen obstacles and crowd spacing. Ordinary AI, dogs, physics,
grenades and the calendar continue to advance.

Measurement
-----------
Linux Release C++17 (-O3 -DNDEBUG), AMD Ryzen 5 7535HS, pinned to CPU 0.
Scenario: scenarios/gray-scott-corridors/scenario.toml, seed 42.
53,000 outdoor zombies (1,000 travellers), 10,000 dormant interior slots,
500 dogs and 1,000 parked cars. Observer feet (-399,-6.45434237,-200), selected
by a deterministic search for safe terrain and 70 m initial actor clearance.
No scenario/map files were modified.

Map SHA256: 80750ed7faaf530fe9f9067bca76b6a075287a7a8ecd827913c1dc9f54d0a734
Probe SHA256: a31d74ca567b236202d3f99da75a75f55ec76afc9e34e49e917ba5573b86cefd

Each run reloads the same seeded scene, warms 120 ticks, then measures 540 ticks
(nine simulation seconds). Three pairs alternate awake and sleeping runs.
Rendering, audio, networking, startup and observer selection are outside timing.
The probe calls production World::step without source overrides, linking fresh
libraries against their current headers. No builds/tests run during measurement.

Median of three run means:
  Awake world update:        1.177836 ms/tick
  Sleeping world update:      .048227 ms/tick
  Simulation cost reduction: 24.42x

The 30x budget is .555556 ms/tick. This short safe-observer case has substantial
CPU headroom (about 346x unthrottled throughput); gameplay is capped at 30x.
This is not a sustained whole-game guarantee: nearby AI, unsettled physics and
different observers can cost more. The modes intentionally use different movement
fidelity and do not claim collision-equivalent trajectories.

An exploratory 120-second sleep attempt naturally woke after roughly nine
simulation seconds when a living threat approached. The comparison uses the same
nine-second window for both modes; all 540 measured sleep ticks remain asleep.
The default calendar maps eight game hours to 400 simulation seconds, so an
uninterrupted 30x sleep targets about 13.3 seconds of wall time.

Validation and artifacts
------------------------
Focused tests cover deadlines, guards, speed transitions, recovery, blocked
movement, membership changes, stale proposals, proximity, crossed path legs,
ignored obstacles, ground restoration, retained routes and presentation-clock
handoff. A UDP session completes half a game hour under acceleration and then
verifies normal pacing. GPU HUD captures cover blackout and manual waking;
the viewer input smoke check verifies a black scene target too.

Local artifacts: build/sleep-runtime-benchmark/
  probe.cpp, build_probe.py, run.py: source and orchestration
  matched-{0,1,2}-{awake,sleep}.log: raw runs
  summary.json, progress.log: timings and hashes

Rebuild the project first, then:
  .venv/bin/python build/sleep-runtime-benchmark/build_probe.py
  .venv/bin/python build/sleep-runtime-benchmark/run.py
