Zombie LOD1/LOD2 image-quality comparison — 2026-10-28
====================================================

Result
------
There is some room for a moderate reduction, but the current near thresholds
are not obviously excessive. A wholesale halving of either mesh introduces
larger silhouette changes at its entry distance.

At 12 m, a 35%-of-original candidate cuts current LOD1 triangles by about 30%,
but raises the median per-view silhouette edge-p95 from 0.50 to 0.75 pixels.
The 25% candidate cuts about 50%, with a larger increase to 1.12 pixels.
At 35 m, the 15% candidate cuts current LOD2 triangles by about 25%, with a
smaller increase from 0.50 to 0.56 pixels. The 10% candidate cuts about 50%,
raising that measure to 0.79 pixels and producing more noticeable thin-limb
and hand changes in some views.

The 15% LOD2 candidate is the stronger starting point for an in-game trial.
The 35% LOD1 candidate is a possible quality/performance tradeoff, while the
25% candidate is too aggressive for an appearance-preserving change at 12 m.
These are engineering/visual judgments about these assets, not user-study
thresholds. No production LOD asset, manifest or distance policy was changed.

First compute the 95th percentile of symmetric nearest boundary-point distance
within each image pair. The median and across-view p95 above then summarize
those 1,920 per-view values. Boundaries include silhouette gaps and holes.
The metric is not vertex displacement or an analytic geometric error bound.

Compared with the current LOD rather than the original, candidate 35% at 12 m
has median edge-p95 0.559 pixels and across-view p95 0.901. Candidate 15% at
35 m has median 0.354 and across-view p95 0.559. Added error can therefore be
distinguished from differences already present in the current meshes.

Some isolated boundary errors are much larger than p95. Even current LOD1 has
a worst symmetric nearest-boundary distance of 6.10 pixels in one sampled view.
Those local outliers are retained in metrics.csv; they are not hidden by the
aggregate table. Inspect representative and worst-case images together.

Workload and protocol
---------------------
10 actual characters x 3 clips (idle/walk/run) x 8 phases x 8 yaw angles x
2 depths (12/35 m) = 3,840 matched views. Each view has eight mesh variants:
original, current LOD1/2/3, and new 35/25/15/10% candidates: 30,720 renders.
All source rigs, nodes and animation data are preserved. Candidates 35/25%
retain LOD1's existing 0.03 simplifier error limit; candidates 15/10% retain
LOD2's 0.08 limit. No error-limit inflation was required to reach their counts.

Camera: level, eye Y=1.65 m, normal 60-degree vertical FOV, 1280x720 projection.
Depth means forward Z distance, slightly less than eye-to-bounds-center range.
Every variant uses the original asset's idle-bound normalization to 1.8 m.
The original idle foreground measured 93.25 pixels high at 12 m and 32 at 35 m
in the first-character validation capture.

Color captures are the same 192x192 crop at full-scene top-left (544,304), with
native raster sampling and authored sRGB materials. Silhouette depth captures
use four-times linear resolution over the identical crop. Boundary distances
are divided by four. Images are never individually fitted, registered or
realigned. Default sunlight is fixed; blending, dithering, shadows, scene
geometry, haze, fog and postprocessing are absent.

The capture uses loadZombieModel and the production basic vertex/fragment
shaders with the intact shader definition. It runs the shared baked-vertex
animation path. All variants use the identical displayed frame on the normal
15 Hz grid at 12 m and 10 Hz grid at 35 m, so this isolates geometry quality.
The eight-pose report cycle is not full-rate playback or a transition test.

Hardware: AMD Radeon 660M, radeonsi/rembrandt, LLVM 19.1.1, DRM 3.61,
Linux 6.11.0-29-generic, Mesa 24.2.8, OpenGL 4.6 Core context.
No frame-time or FPS improvement was measured. Geometry/vertex-count savings
do not establish an equivalent frame-time gain.
