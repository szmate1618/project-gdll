Corridor chase CPU rendering investigation
Recorded 2026-10-27. Release build; Radeon 660M, Mesa 24.2.8, hardware OpenGL.

Finding and change
Zombie camera visibility gathered and sorted distant candidates before applying
the camera draw-distance policy. A short diagnostic run attributed about 7.3 ms
to the visibility query and another 1.0 ms to LOD selection, despite only about
100 submitted camera instances. Palette uploads and GL error checking were tiny.

The renderer now supplies a conservative camera region derived from the existing
distance policy and projection scale. Group and member bounds outside that region
skip camera frustum/candidate work. Full transformed bounds remain authoritative;
exact center-distance/LOD decisions still run on surviving candidates. Zoom
extends the region, and disabled detail/unlimited distance omit it entirely.
Sun and flashlight membership remain independent of camera distance. Simulation,
collision, pursuit lifetime and actor activation were not changed.

Matched walking replay
- Existing published town and vegetation; no generation or map changes.
- Frozen snapshot of the user's corridor configuration: seed 42, 53,000 actors
  (52,000 initially idle and 1,000 travelers), cropped density image, player
  spawn X=-700 Z=300, yaw=-142.5, pitch=0.
- Hidden hardware rendering at 1280x720; normal shadows/LOD, audio enabled,
  default day/night cycle, no shooting, no ragdolls. Vehicles and birds retained.
- Exactly 4,800 fixed 1/60 s steps: 80 simulated seconds per case.
- First 60 steps stationary; then W+A walking at 3 m/s. If progress over a
  second falls below 1.5 m, sidestep left for three seconds to bypass buildings.
  Input replay is in scratch main snapshots, not production gameplay code.
- Before and after each run the same route, with perception enabled and disabled.
- Fine zombie profiling enabled in all four cases. It adds overhead: the earlier
  short control pair measured about 0.77 ms/step of instrumentation. These are
  matched diagnostic timings, not uninstrumented gameplay performance claims.
- Table uses medians over frames 3600..4800 inclusive (the final 20 seconds).

Case                         CPU simulation   CPU render   Total CPU work
Before, perception on           4.884 ms       10.512 ms      15.630 ms
After, perception on            4.873 ms        6.978 ms      12.068 ms
Before, perception off          4.100 ms       10.481 ms      14.781 ms
After, perception off           4.117 ms        7.024 ms      11.356 ms

For the chase case, median CPU render fell 33.6%; render p95 fell from 11.941
to 7.644 ms. Simulation stayed essentially unchanged. Perception/chasing added
about 0.8 ms of simulation in this late interval, independently of the render
improvement. Earlier 6 m/s replays accumulated about 200 chasing actors after
80 seconds; ordinary gameplay behavior took about 1.2 ms/step there.

Both before/after final screenshot pairs are pixel-identical. The chase pair
retains 44 camera instances, camera LOD0/1/2/3 counts 6/3/12/23, 20,590 camera
triangles and 26,430 sun-shadow triangles. Captures were visually inspected.

The original 39-41 FPS gunfire/ragdoll intervals were not reproduced by this
walking replay. This establishes a substantial CPU render bottleneck and its
improvement, not that every reported dip has one cause. Off-screen pursuers
still simulate and may cast shadows. New inclusive behavior/travel timings,
state counts, and coarse CPU render/simulation stages help distinguish those
costs in a future interactive trace. Inclusive/nested timings must not be added.

Validation
- Full Release build.
- Focused zombie visibility, LOD, movement, behavior, chase and travel tests.
- Hardware zombie renderer regressions, including camera distance rejection,
  scoped rendering and independent shadow/palette retention.
- Visibility tests compare with an independent per-instance scan, including
  range-boundary contact, wide bounds, moved groups and flashlight-only actors.
- Required zombies-open-100k-v1 control: all six rounds submitted all 100,000
  actors, 10 draws and 12,580,000 camera triangles; distance culling disabled.
  Median of round means: GPU 16.765 ms, CPU render 20.446 ms. This control checks
  the unlimited-view case; it is not a before/after timing comparison.
