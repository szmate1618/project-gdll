October 19/20 source and retained-binary comparison — 2026-10-28
================================================================

Finding
-------
There is no zombie-rendering optimization between the last October 19 revision
and the original fast October 20 benchmark revision. The earlier comparison
mixed source dates with measurement dates: October 19 source was rebuilt and
measured on October 28, while the faster October 20 records were measured on
October 20 itself.

Six new mirrored runs compare the October 19 rebuild, a retained October 20
original executable, and the committed shader split in one session. All use
2048-square sun maps. Median of two process results, milliseconds:

Binary                              CPU render        GPU elapsed
October 19 rebuild                     19.176             13.935
Retained October 20 original            19.285             14.435
Current shader split, 08e3694           19.239             13.423

CPU differences are smaller than observed run variation. Current CPU rendering
is effectively comparable with both old binaries in this session. The retained
October 20 executable also does not reproduce the earlier 17.662-18.468 ms CPU
records today. The repository history does not identify a missing October 20
code optimization responsible for that historical absolute timing difference.
Runtime conditions outside these source changes remain a hypothesis; this
experiment does not isolate a particular clock, power or background-load cause.

Protocol, workload and results
-------------------------------
Order: October 19 / retained October 20 / current / current / retained October 20 /
October 19. Every process uses six rounds of 32 warmup and 64 measured frames
with the same deterministic 60 Hz simulation/animation timeline. Each yields
384 measured frames and 384 finite GPU samples. The standard summarizer reports
the median of six round means per process, then this report takes the median
of the two process results per binary.

0 October 19                           19.089             14.286

2 current                              18.951             13.422

All runs use the Radeon 660M, Mesa 24.2.8-1ubuntu1~24.10.1, OpenGL 4.6 Core,
1280x720, the same assets/camera, 100,000 LOD3 actors, 10 camera draws and
12,580,000 camera triangles. Zombie distance removal is disabled. No zombie
sun-shadow casters, active AI behaviors, audio, player movement or dynamic
physics are present. Asset fingerprints and every common metadata field match;
newer optional fields remain explicitly absent in old metadata.

All six captures are byte-identical, SHA-256:
64df6d5b8d42d4ade6788a66a25ae0bf048bf3809f9dc4205dc3ffdcb148d473
All rounds complete their population checks and GL error checks.

Before each process the runner checks for competing known build/benchmark jobs,
pauses five seconds and waits for three consecutive zero GPU-activity readings
at 0.5-second intervals. Read-only telemetry runs every 0.5 seconds. No clocks
or power settings were locked. Post-run inspection reports AC online/balanced;
that is not continuous power logging. Temperature spans 48-67 C and available
RAM 7.50-9.47 GiB. There are two process repetitions per binary; GPU/CPU overlap,
driver waits and scheduling variability prevent precise general speed claims.

Global telemetry records 104,816 swap-out pages, about 409 MiB, during startup
of the first October 19 invocation. These increments finish before its initial
capture, which precedes warmup and measured rounds. Later sampled counters in
that invocation show zero swap-out and one page in. The full session records
116 swap-in pages (464 KiB). Full-invocation memory pressure includes startup;
it must not be assigned wholesale to measured rendering. Raw telemetry and
capture timestamps preserve this distinction without claiming exact per-frame
pressure instrumentation. The final host audit finds no remaining benchmark
or build processes.

Single retained-October-20 run after closing Firefox
----------------------------------------------------
At the user's request, Firefox's dedicated user-session application scope was
stopped, and all 48 processes in that scope were confirmed exited. Direct signal
and desktop-interface requests from this environment were blocked by its Snap
policy; the session manager performed the shutdown. No other apps were closed.

Exactly one invocation of the same retained October 20 executable then ran
with its own shaders and native 2048 sun map: the usual six rounds of 32 warmup
and 64 measured frames, fixed 60 Hz timeline, 384 finite GPU samples.

Observation                          CPU render        GPU elapsed
Earlier Firefox-open, two-run median    19.285             14.435
Firefox closed, one later process       18.568             13.140

The later result is 0.717 ms (3.7%) lower CPU render and 1.295 ms (9.0%) lower
GPU elapsed than the preceding two-run median. It is also below both preceding
process results, whose ranges were CPU 18.898-19.672 / GPU 13.754-15.116 ms.
This is one later observation after app shutdown, not a paired, repeated
Firefox-open/closed experiment; it cannot isolate the whole difference to
Firefox. No extra benchmark invocations were made for this request.

Available RAM increased from 9.32 GiB immediately before shutdown to 15.48 GiB
immediately after it, and settled at 18.80-19.73 GiB during the benchmark.
The preceding Firefox-open runs had 8.30-9.45 GiB available. The new invocation
records zero swap-in and swap-out pages. All 100,000 LOD3 actors, 10 draws and
12,580,000 triangles remain submitted. Its capture hash is unchanged from the
six-run comparison above. Firefox and benchmark processes remain stopped.
