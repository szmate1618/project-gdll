Torso wound shader, cavity physics and neck settling investigation
Recorded 2026-10-23. Baseline source: 05b64db.

Findings
The controlled runs did not show a repeatable GPU regression from the shared
wound shader. Cavities added measurable CPU work, approximately 0.17-0.30 ms
per frame for 20 cavities and 60 loose objects in this fixture. These numbers
do not reproduce or fully explain the reported change from stable 60 to 49 FPS.

During the investigation, both CPU and GPU timings became substantially faster
without a change in the reported power source or platform profile. AC remained
offline, the battery was discharging, and the ACPI platform profile was balanced.
This prevents treating all runs as one timing distribution. It does not identify
which clock, power, thermal, scheduling or background-load condition changed.

Separately, a real-asset physics probe reproduced ragdolls failing to sleep.
A zero-speed neck motor limited to 4 N*m let all 20 probe ragdolls sleep naturally
within five simulated seconds. Before the change, all 20 remained awake after
60 simulated seconds. This is a reproducible settling improvement, not a measured
end-to-end gameplay FPS improvement.

- All cases retain the same 20 ragdolls, source assets, draw counts and LODs.
- Each of those 20 actors receives a nonempty all-zero wound mask. This exercises
  mask lookup/original-LOD selection while retaining identical visible surfaces.
- Original shader control removes wound varyings, uniforms and fragment logic

  modified for these comparisons. The renderer and geometry are otherwise shared.
- Cavity control adds 20 intact inner shells and their 60 dynamic props using
  the same production ZombieTorsoPhysics implementation. These diagnostic props
  are not submitted for rendering, isolating the physics cost. Kinematic shells
  follow the simulated torsos and prepare streamed ground collision normally.
- Scenario route movement is frozen; perception/audio are disabled. Fixed 1/60 s
  simulation steps, displayed animation frozen at zero, time of day 12.75 frozen.
- 480 frames per run; first 60 excluded. CPU medians use 420 frames. GPU timestamp
  medians use available samples only: 336-368 valid GPU samples of those 420.
  The 16-slot asynchronous query ring can skip samples; missing samples are never
  counted as zero. No per-frame glFinish is used; final draining is outside timing.
- Three alternating four-case rounds, followed by one extra reverse-order round
  after the timing regime changed. Sixteen runs total, all on battery (75% to 71%).
- Each final screenshot is byte-identical: SHA256
  f17d477e04970dcd272cc08f6746acf16f62b000ec17bd05c62ee6b62e49b4c8
  Verify against summary.json for the exact retained hash.
- Final counts in every case: 2,449 trees, eight tree draws; six town draws;
  100 visible zombies, 20 ragdolls, 20 zombie draws; LOD0/1/2/3 = 20/80/0/0;

Sleep probe and selected change
The separate CPU-only probe loads all ten real source characters, uses each
twice with different idle phases, normalizes height to 1.8 m, and spaces the
20 bodies eight meters apart. Each receives the normal mapped ragdoll activation
with {0,0,-4} input velocity. Their own artificial floors remain in use. No cavity
physics, animation-driven pose overwrite or visual inference determines sleep:
ZombieRagdollWorld::sleeping() checks every body's actual Box3D awake state.

Before: 0/20 sleeping at 1, 5, 15, 30 and 60 simulated seconds.
Neck motor, zero relative angular velocity, 4 N*m: 20/20 sleeping by five seconds.
8 N*m: 19/20 by five seconds; one remained awake at 60 seconds.
12 N*m: 18/20 by five seconds; existing detached-part settling check failed.
12 N*m plus head angular drag 8/s: 19/20; settling check passed.
4 N*m plus head angular drag 8/s: 19/20; settling check passed.

Selected 4 N*m without extra world-space head drag: it settled all real-asset
probe cases and passed the existing detached-part settling checks. The motor
opposes relative head/torso rotation; it adds no restoring spring or target pose.
Neck severing destroys that joint, so detached head spin is unaffected. Other
ragdoll joints, launch speeds and sleep thresholds retain their existing policy.
This fixture does not guarantee every pile or externally disturbed corpse sleeps.

Process/power snapshot
After benchmarking, host top reported approximately 92% CPU idle, about 20 GiB
available RAM, no swap use and no lingering viewer/benchmark processes. VS Code,
Codex and the desktop had some activity; the snapshot did not show a runaway
background CPU job. This does not rule out earlier transient background load.
A brief read-only GPU-clock sampler was not synchronized to individual draws,
so its clock readings are not evidence of the clocks during each GPU interval.
No power, clock, process or hardware settings were changed by this investigation.
